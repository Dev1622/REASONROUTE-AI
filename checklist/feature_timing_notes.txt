ReasonRoute AI - Feature Timing And Positioning Notes

Purpose:
Capture the add-on feature ideas discussed, explain why they matter, and specify when each should be built so the product stays focused on its real core: explainable AI routing that reduces unnecessary overrides.

Core product rule:
Do not let add-on features change the product's aim.
The center of gravity must remain:
- explain the chosen route
- show tradeoffs clearly
- help dispatchers trust the recommendation
- reduce blind overrides
- collect structured learning signals for phase 2

========================================
BUILD NOW FOR SHADOW PILOT
========================================

1. Override Cost Delta

What it is:
When a dispatcher requests an adjustment, show the cost delta before they confirm it.
Example:
"Your adjusted route saves 10 mins, but adds +14 miles and +$22 in fuel costs."

Why it fits now:
- Directly supports the current product story.
- Helps fleet managers see the business cost of overrides.
- Makes dispatchers pause and think like owners.
- Can be built on top of route comparison logic without needing live telemetry.

When to build:
- Immediate shadow pilot build candidate.
- High priority.

Notes:
- Keep the message simple and business-facing.
- Show time, miles, and estimated cost delta.
- Do not block the dispatcher from adjusting; just make the tradeoff visible.

2. Tribal Knowledge Tags

What it is:
When the dispatcher requests an adjustment, let them click fast tags instead of typing everything manually.
Examples:
- School Zone Traffic
- Trailer Weight / Bridge Issue
- Customer Dock is Hard to Back Into
- Driver Preference

Why it fits now:
- Very low friction.
- Feels native to logistics workflows.
- Creates structured data that will power phase 2 learning later.
- Improves the current review and adjustment flow without requiring major infrastructure.

When to build:
- Immediate shadow pilot build candidate.
- High priority.

Notes:
- This is one of the cleanest bridges from phase 1 to phase 2.
- Use fixed tags first, then allow optional note text.

3. Phone Call Killer

What it is:
Generate a one-line driver-facing explanation and include it in the driver manifest or route handoff.
Example:
"Route updated. Sent via 4th St. to avoid a 42-minute accident delay on Highway 95."

Why it fits now:
- Extends explainability down to the driver level.
- Reduces confusion and likely reduces clarification calls.
- Can be implemented as an export-level feature first without building a full driver app.

When to build:
- Good shadow pilot add-on.
- Medium-high priority.

Notes:
- Keep it very short and easy to understand.
- Do not expose raw AI reasoning or internal optimization language.

========================================
BUILD LATER - PHASE 2 / LIVE OPS
========================================

4. Blast Radius Reroute

What it is:
If a truck is delayed, highlight downstream SLA failures and propose a rescue plan.
Example:
"Truck 4 is delayed by 90 mins. This will cause 3 downstream SLA failures. Proposal: Move Stops B and C to Truck 7."

Why it is strong:
- Turns the dispatcher into a proactive strategist.
- Makes the system explain not just routes, but recovery actions.
- Extremely powerful buyer story for exception management.

Why not now:
- Requires real delay input.
- Requires SLA logic.
- Requires cross-vehicle reassignment logic.
- Requires a notion of downstream operational failure.

When to build:
- Later phase build.
- Phase 2 or after shadow pilot success.

5. Driver Empathy Score

What it is:
Show a driver impact tag alongside route efficiency metrics.
Example:
"Driver fatigue avoided. Assigned to Smith because this keeps him under his allowed weekly hours and back to depot earlier."

Why it is strong:
- Great buyer narrative.
- Signals that the system balances efficiency with retention and driver wellbeing.

Why not now:
- Needs real HOS / driver hours / shift history.
- Needs actual driver assignment context.
- Risky if presented before the data is truly modeled.

When to build:
- Later phase build.
- After real driver state and compliance inputs exist.

6. Planned vs Actual Replay

What it is:
Compare AI recommendation vs dispatcher override using actual GPS results and show who was right.
Example:
"You overrode the AI 12 times this week. 8 times you were right. 4 times the AI was right."

Why it is strong:
- Builds trust through evidence.
- Turns dispatcher learning into a measurable loop.
- Great for weekly ops review and adoption.

Why not now:
- Requires actual GPS data.
- Requires saved override history.
- Requires planned vs actual route replay logic.

When to build:
- Later phase build.
- After live driver tracking exists.

7. Customer-Facing Shield

What it is:
Push a sanitized explanation to the end customer when ETA changes.
Example:
"Your delivery ETA is now 2:15 PM. Reason: rerouted due to severe weather on I-95. Driver is taking the fastest safe alternative."

Why it is strong:
- Reduces angry customer calls.
- Makes delays easier to accept because the customer understands why.
- Can fit SMS / WhatsApp / tracking-link workflows.

Why not now:
- Needs a notification pipeline.
- Needs customer-facing ETA state.
- Needs external messaging integration.

When to build:
- Later phase build.
- Could begin as a pilot extension after dispatcher-side shadow pilot is proven.

========================================

Best fit overall by current product leverage:
1. Override Cost Delta
2. Tribal Knowledge Tags
3. Phone Call Killer
4. Customer-Facing Shield
5. Planned vs Actual Replay
6. Blast Radius Reroute
7. Driver Empathy Score

Recommended next build order:
- Override Cost Delta
- Tribal Knowledge Tags
- Driver-facing one-line explanation export

Reason:
This combination improves the current shadow-pilot story, helps the buyer, helps the dispatcher, and creates structured data for phase 2 without needing live driver telemetry.

========================================
FACT CHECK / CLAIM TIGHTENING
========================================

Use with confidence:
- Explanations can increase trust in algorithmic recommendations.
- Plain-language rationale is part of the core product value.
- HOS constraints are real and useful, but must be modeled carefully.

Use carefully / soften wording:
- "Replacing a driver costs $8,000 to $12,000"
  Better: replacing a driver can cost thousands to low five figures depending on recruiting, training, downtime, and lost productivity.

- "Dispatchers spend 30% of their day on the phone"
  Better: dispatchers lose significant time to driver clarification calls and exception handling.

- "This saves two hours a day"
  Better: this can reduce clarification calls and exception-handling time.

HOS caution:
- Do not present a single universal rule like "60 hours" as if it applies in every case.
- For U.S. property-carrying drivers, FMCSA rules depend on the 60/70 hour and 7/8 day operating context.

========================================
POSITIONING RULES
========================================

Good add-on features:
- strengthen explainability
- quantify tradeoffs
- reduce blind overrides
- create structured signals for future learning

Bad add-on features:
- distract from the core route decision story
- depend on live operational data that the product does not yet have
- force big infrastructure work before shadow pilot proof exists

Final guidance:
For shadow pilot, build only the add-ons that make the dispatcher trust loop sharper.
For later phases, build the live operational features only after real tracking, SLA state, and feedback history exist.
