MOAT AND PROOF NOTES

How we arrived at the thesis:
- Dispatchers are the final gatekeepers before routes reach drivers.
- In current systems, they review routes and manually override by changing assignment or stop sequence.
- They usually do not redraw turn-by-turn driving paths.
- Once they override, the system typically just accepts the forced sequence and recalculates ETA and mileage.
- The software does not explain the tradeoff or show the cost of the change.

This is the core workflow truth:
- Black box route recommendation
- Manual drag-and-drop override
- No cost visibility
- No structured knowledge capture

That creates the Trust-Profit Gap:
- Dispatchers protect service outcomes using intuition.
- The business loses margin because the cost of those changes is hidden.

Core proof-backed product claims we can stand behind:
- Legacy routing workflows are generally review-and-approve, not full blind automation.
- Manual overrides commonly happen through assignment and sequencing.
- Existing software usually recalculates the locked route rather than negotiating the decision with the dispatcher.
- The middle layer between "accept" and "override" is underbuilt.

Claims that are strong as thesis but still need careful wording:
- "No major player explains routes in a dispatcher-facing reasoning UI."
- "We are the first Glass Box."
- "Every override widens the moat."

How to speak about them:
- Use them as positioning statements, not unqualified market facts.
- Prefer language like:
  - "Current tools largely act like black boxes"
  - "We have not seen commercial dispatcher-first reasoning UIs become standard"
  - "Our moat grows as dispatcher corrections become structured training data"

The moat, clearly:
1. Tribal Knowledge Data Moat
2. Workflow Integration Moat
3. Trust-First Brand Moat
4. Human Feedback Flywheel

Best YC-style phrasing:
- Competitors are building robots to replace the dispatcher.
- We are building an exoskeleton to empower them.
- Our moat is not the code.
- Our moat is the proprietary dataset of human logistics intuition captured through negotiated route decisions.
