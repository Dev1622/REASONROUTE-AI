ROUTING PLUS GLASS BOX PRODUCT DECISION

Purpose:
- Lock the updated product decision clearly before further UI and implementation work.
- Resolve whether ReasonRoute should behave like a thin negotiation layer, a pure routing dashboard, or a combined product.

Final decision:
- ReasonRoute will be built as a real routing product and dispatcher console, with the Glass Box decision layer built into its core workflow.
- We are not keeping routing in the background.
- We are not treating explanation and negotiation as a thin add-on over someone else’s system.
- We are building a product that can actually run a fleet in a shadow pilot with real organizational data, while also exposing the decision, tradeoff, and learning layer that makes ReasonRoute different.

Why this decision is correct:
- Many organizations may not allow us to integrate only as a thin review layer on top of their existing routing software in the first pilot stage.
- To run a real shadow pilot, we need to generate routes, display them credibly, let dispatchers review and adjust them, and export evidence from our own product.
- That means routing is not just supporting infrastructure for now; it is part of the product surface.
- At the same time, if we let the product drift into a generic route planner, we lose the real wedge.
- Therefore, the right decision is to combine both identities into one coherent system.

What ReasonRoute is now:
1. A real routing and fleet-planning product
2. A dispatcher review and negotiation console
3. A Glass Box explanation and tradeoff system
4. A shadow-pilot-ready product for real organizational data

What ReasonRoute is not:
- Not just a routing optimizer with a decorative explanation panel
- Not just a thin AI review layer sitting on top of another routing stack
- Not just a generic logistics operations dashboard

Core product principle:
- Routing must be first-class.
- Negotiation must be first-class.
- The product must do both without collapsing into clutter.

UI consequence of this decision:
- Before route generation, the product should feel like a capable routing and fleet-planning system.
- After route generation, the product should shift its center of gravity toward proposal review, explanation, tradeoff visibility, and dispatcher action.
- This means the UI is stateful by intent:
  - planning mode before optimization
  - decision mode after optimization
- The routing product does not disappear after optimization.
- The negotiation layer becomes primary at the moment the dispatcher must review or change a route.

Working product model:
- The map stays central because this is a real routing product.
- Setup, intake, and fleet controls are legitimate product features and should feel serious and usable.
- Once a proposal exists, the proposal, explanation, dispatcher action, and cost-delta flow should become the most visually dominant layer.
- Secondary evidence, diagnostics, and exports should support that decision, not compete with it.

Implication for the six-phase implementation plan:
- The six-phase implementation plan remains valid.
- We are not shrinking the product down to only the negotiation layer.
- We are building the full routing workflow needed for a true shadow pilot.
- But every phase must still preserve the Glass Box thesis:
  - explain
  - negotiate
  - quantify
  - learn

Guiding rule going forward:
- If a feature strengthens real routing execution for pilot credibility, it is valid.
- If a feature strengthens the decision layer, it is valid.
- If a feature does neither and only adds generic enterprise complexity, it should be deprioritized.

Best one-line summary:
- ReasonRoute is a real fleet-routing product with a Glass Box dispatcher decision layer at its core.

Best UI summary:
- Build a real routing dashboard that automatically transitions into a decision-first negotiation console once routes are generated.
