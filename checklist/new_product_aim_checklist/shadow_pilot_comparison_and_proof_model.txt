SHADOW PILOT COMPARISON AND PROOF MODEL

Purpose:
- Define exactly how the ReasonRoute shadow pilot should work with real organizational historical data.
- Clarify what we are comparing, what we are proving, and what evidence matters to the organization owner.

Core shadow pilot model:
- The organization provides past real operational input data for a specific day, shift, route set, or delivery workload.
- We run that same workload through ReasonRoute.
- ReasonRoute generates route proposals, route metrics, explanations, and decision evidence.
- We compare ReasonRoute's output not only against its own baseline, but also against what the organization actually did for that workload.
- The goal is not only to prove better routing math.
- The goal is to prove better routing decision quality.

What a real shadow pilot means here:
- We are not just demoing a route planner.
- We are replaying historical operational reality.
- We are examining how routing decisions were made, how overrides happened, and what cost or margin impact came from those decisions.
- We are showing what ReasonRoute would have recommended, explained, preserved, changed, or avoided.

========================================
1. INPUT MODEL FOR SHADOW PILOT
========================================

The shadow pilot should use the organization's past real data where possible.

Primary historical input:
- Stops / orders / depot / vehicle context for a specific operating day or scenario
- Fleet count and relevant vehicle identity where available
- Real route or dispatch outcome for that same workload if available
- Real final metrics if available:
  - total distance
  - total route time
  - vehicles used
  - fuel cost if known
  - emissions if known
  - service or SLA outcome if known

Important rule:
- The same workload must be evaluated across both the organization's historical outcome and ReasonRoute's generated proposal.
- Comparison must be like-for-like enough to be operationally credible.

========================================
2. WHAT REASONROUTE SHOULD GENERATE
========================================

When the Plan Fleet button is used on historical organizational data, ReasonRoute should generate:
- Fleet route proposals
- Route metrics
- Road-snapped route evidence
- Why This Route explanation
- Decision summary
- Adjustment preview capability
- Override cost delta
- Negotiated alternative evaluation where applicable
- Pilot evidence outputs

This means ReasonRoute acts as:
1. a real routing system
2. a fleet review console
3. a decision and override analysis system

========================================
3. WHAT WE ARE ACTUALLY COMPARING
========================================

The shadow pilot should compare at least three layers when possible.

Layer A - ReasonRoute System Baseline
- Internal baseline generated by ReasonRoute for the workload
- Useful for consistent internal comparison

Layer B - ReasonRoute Proposed Outcome
- ReasonRoute's main recommended route set and decision evidence

Layer C - Organization Historical Outcome
- What the organization actually ran for that same workload
- This is the key buyer-trust benchmark

Important comparison principle:
- The product should not rely only on comparing its optimized route against its own internally generated baseline.
- It should also compare against the organization's real past result whenever that data is available.

========================================
4. WHAT THE PILOT MUST PROVE
========================================

The pilot should not stop at proving:
- lower distance
- lower ETA
- lower emissions

Those are useful, but not the full value story.

The pilot should prove:
1. How many overrides happened in the organization's real workflow
2. Which overrides were operationally essential
3. Which overrides should have been avoided
4. How often the original system or planned route was already correct
5. How often the dispatcher was correct to reject or change the route
6. Whether the dispatcher's chosen override was truly the best alternative
7. Whether another better alternative existed and was effectively missed
8. How the override happened:
   - assignment change
   - sequencing change
   - reroute due to disruption
   - local tribal knowledge intervention
9. How much cost the override introduced
10. How much should have been saved under a better negotiated decision
11. How much loss or margin leakage happened that day because of cost-blind decision behavior

This is the real owner-level proof.

========================================
5. THE KEY PRODUCT THESIS INSIDE THE PILOT
========================================

The pilot must reflect the real ReasonRoute thesis:
- Existing routing systems may already be strong at route computation.
- The deeper gap is in explanation, negotiation, and cost-aware decision support.
- Dispatchers often act under time pressure.
- They may keep the route, override it, or force a change without full tradeoff visibility.
- Sometimes the original route was actually right.
- Sometimes the dispatcher was right.
- Sometimes neither was truly best, and a better alternative existed.

ReasonRoute must prove that it improves this decision layer.

========================================
6. WHAT SHOULD BE SHOWN TO THE OWNER
========================================

The owner should receive evidence in business terms.

Core proof outputs:
- What the organization ran
- What ReasonRoute would have proposed
- Where they matched
- Where they diverged
- Which historical overrides were justified
- Which overrides caused avoidable cost
- Whether better alternatives existed
- Estimated margin leakage from override behavior
- Estimated savings from better negotiated decisions

Important owner-facing framing:
- We are not just claiming "our route is shorter."
- We are proving where money was lost in the decision workflow and how it could have been prevented.

========================================
7. WHAT SHOULD BE SHOWN TO THE DISPATCHER OR OPS TEAM
========================================

Dispatcher and operations proof should include:
- Proposed route and explanation
- Comparison with historical route outcome
- Cost delta for likely override scenarios
- Cases where historical behavior was right
- Cases where the route system was already right
- Cases where a third alternative would have been better
- Structured capture of the likely reason behind historical override behavior

This makes the pilot credible and non-accusatory.

Important principle:
- The pilot should not treat dispatchers as "wrong by default."
- The pilot should treat them as operating under real pressure, with incomplete visibility.
- The product's job is to improve the decision quality around that pressure.

========================================
8. BASELINE MODEL DECISION
========================================

ReasonRoute should support both:
1. Internal system baseline
2. Organization historical baseline

Why both matter:
- Internal baseline helps evaluate the engine consistently
- Historical organizational baseline helps prove real-world credibility

Preferred maturity path:
Level 1:
- Historical metrics-only baseline
- Distance, time, vehicles, fuel/emissions if available

Level 2:
- Historical route-level baseline
- Vehicle assignment and sequence if available

Level 3:
- Historical geometry-backed baseline
- Road-level or exported route shape if available

========================================
9. SUCCESS CONDITION FOR THE SHADOW PILOT
========================================

A successful pilot should allow us to say:
- We processed real organizational historical data successfully.
- We generated real route proposals and metrics.
- We compared against the organization's real past outcome.
- We identified where decision behavior created avoidable cost or margin leakage.
- We showed where the system was correct, where the dispatcher was correct, and where a better alternative existed.
- We quantified savings not only from route optimization, but from better routing decisions.

========================================
10. FINAL SHADOW PILOT RULE
========================================

The shadow pilot is not just a routing benchmark.
It is a historical decision-analysis replay.

ReasonRoute must prove:
- route quality
- explanation quality
- override cost visibility
- decision improvement
- structured learning opportunity

Best one-line summary:
- A true ReasonRoute shadow pilot replays historical organizational routing reality and proves how better explanation, negotiation, and cost-aware alternatives would have changed routing decisions and reduced silent margin leakage.
