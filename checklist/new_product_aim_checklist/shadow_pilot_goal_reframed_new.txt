SHADOW PILOT GOAL REFRAMED

Old shadow-pilot framing:
- Prove we can generate better optimized routes from real organizational data.

New shadow-pilot framing:
- Prove that ReasonRoute exposes the real decision gap inside dispatcher workflow.
- Show that dispatchers are often forced to make fast route decisions under time pressure, with SLA and ETA pressure dominating the moment.
- Show that this usually becomes a false binary:
  keep the proposed route, or manually override it quickly.
- Show that this override moment is where silent margin leakage begins:
  the dispatcher is trying to protect service, not run a cost audit.
- Show that neither side of the binary is guaranteed to be best:
  the original route may still be the best option on total-cost and constraint logic,
  and the dispatcher's override may solve the immediate concern but still not be the best alternative.
- Show that there may be a better third option:
  an explained, negotiated route that balances ETA, cost, and real-world constraints more intelligently than either the untouched plan or the manual override.

What the shadow pilot must demonstrate:
1. Real route plans are reviewed by dispatchers before dispatch.
2. Dispatchers do not have the time or visibility to deeply evaluate every route decision.
3. Current workflow pushes them toward a fast operational choice:
   keep the route or override it to protect ETA/SLA.
4. Manual overrides usually happen through assignment and sequencing changes.
5. Legacy tools may recalculate ETA or mileage after an override, but they do not create a real decision layer:
   they do not explain the route clearly,
   they do not show explicit cost tradeoffs in the override moment,
   and they do not help compare the original route, the manual override, and a better alternative.
6. ReasonRoute provides the missing middle layer:
   explanation, negotiation, cost delta, alternative proposal generation, and structured reason capture.

What success looks like:
- We can ingest real organizational data.
- We can show the proposed route and explain why it was chosen.
- We can help the dispatcher evaluate whether the current route should be kept.
- We can let the dispatcher request an adjustment instead of blindly dragging stops.
- We can show the operational and financial cost delta of that adjustment.
- We can generate a better negotiated alternative when both the original plan and the manual override are weak.
- We can log why the dispatcher changed it.
- We can quantify the difference between:
  blind acceptance,
  cost-blind manual override,
  and explained negotiated alternatives.

What the shadow pilot should not become:
- A giant enterprise-routing rebuild.
- A feature race against FarEye, Locus, or full TMS suites.
- A pure optimization benchmark with no human workflow story.
- A claim that dispatchers are wrong by default.
- A claim that the original route is always right.

The real output of the pilot:
- Proof that the explanation is the product.
- Proof that cost delta changes dispatcher behavior.
- Proof that the key workflow gap is not just route quality, but decision quality under time pressure.
- Proof that tribal knowledge can be captured as structured input rather than lost in drag-and-drop overrides.
- Proof that ReasonRoute can help the dispatcher evaluate three possibilities:
  keep the current route,
  accept the manual change,
  or move to a better negotiated alternative.
