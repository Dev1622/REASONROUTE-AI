PILOT ADD ON BEFORE DISCUSSION

Purpose:
Capture the product discussion around what must be added before the shadow pilot feels operationally credible with real dispatchers and real organizational data.

Core idea:
The product should not just accept stop name, lat, and lng. A credible shadow pilot must begin to respect the kinds of messy, real operational constraints dispatchers actually work with.

Why this matters:
If ReasonRoute ignores real-world operational fields, the routes may look mathematically clean but will feel broken to a dispatcher.

========================================
PRIORITY 1 - THE NON-NEGOTIABLES
Build these for the May 24 pilots.
If these are missing, the routes will be fundamentally broken in the eyes of a dispatcher.
========================================

1. STOP SERVICE TIME (DWELL TIME)

The logic:
- If a truck has 20 stops and each takes 15 minutes to unload, that is 5 hours of non-driving time.

The gap:
- The engine currently treats stops as taking 0 minutes.
- ETAs will be too early.
- Routes will be over-scheduled.

Why it matters:
- This is one of the fastest ways to lose dispatcher trust.

2. RAW ADDRESS GEOCODING

The logic:
- Real organizations will not hand over clean lat/lng.
- They will give addresses like:
  - "123 Industrial Way, Suite B"
  - "Gate 4, behind the mall, NY 10001"

The gap:
- A middleware layer is needed in geminiService.ts or a dedicated geocodingService.ts.
- It should call a real geocoding API such as Mapbox or Google before the data reaches the routing engine.

Why it matters:
- If the dispatcher must manually convert addresses to coordinates, the pilot becomes painful before it even begins.

3. STRICT TIME WINDOWS (SLA BASICS)

The logic:
- Some stops must be delivered inside a hard window such as 08:00 to 10:00.

The gap:
- There is a type-level notion of time windows, but not enough real enforcement.
- If the AI proposes a stop at 11:00 for a 10:00 deadline, the system must clearly call that out.

Why it matters:
- The reasoning log should explicitly surface things like:
  - SLA violation
  - infeasible arrival
  - reroute required

4. DISPATCHER NOTES PASSTHROUGH

The logic:
- Dispatchers preserve important operational notes such as:
  - gate code
  - rear dock only
  - call security before arrival

The gap:
- CSV parsing and export flow must capture these notes and preserve them all the way through to the manifest and HTML export.

Why it matters:
- This is operational truth, not cosmetic metadata.

========================================
PRIORITY 2 - ENTERPRISE CREDIBILITY LAYER
These prove the product can handle mid-market complexity beyond a simple van-routing demo.
========================================

5. MULTI-DIMENSIONAL CAPACITY (WEIGHT VS VOLUME)

The logic:
- A truck can be full because it is too heavy or because it is physically out of space.

The gap:
- The current system uses a single vehicleCapacity scalar.
- It should evolve toward a richer structure such as:
  - weightKg
  - volumeM3
  - pallets

Why it matters:
- This is one of the clearest signs of operational maturity.

6. VEHICLE ATTRIBUTES (THE REEFER MATCH)

The logic:
- If a stop requires refrigeration, it cannot be assigned to a dry van.
- Similar logic applies to liftgate, flatbed, and other vehicle capabilities.

The gap:
- A matching filter is needed in reasonRouteEngine.ts so infeasible vehicles are excluded or penalized.

Why it matters:
- Without this, the product can generate routes that look good but fail in reality.

7. BASIC HOS (SHIFT LEGALITY)

The logic:
- No route should exceed the driver’s legal drive limit.
- A simple first rule is enough for now, such as an 11-hour hard drive-time cutoff.

The gap:
- The engine needs a basic legality check and route cutoff behavior.

Why it matters:
- Even a first-pass HOS legality layer makes the system feel much more real to operators.

========================================
PRIORITY 3 - THE BILLION-DOLLAR VISION
Do not build these yet.
These are future-phase items, not immediate shadow-pilot blockers.
========================================

8. PICKUP VS DROPOFF STATE MODEL
- Needed to model how a truck gets lighter and heavier throughout the day.
- Important for reverse logistics and more advanced route state.

9. HAZMAT COMPATIBILITY
- Needed for regulated goods and compatibility constraints.
- High-value later, but not mandatory for the immediate pilot.

10. SLA PENALTY COSTING
- Needed to rank stops by financial pain if missed.
- Great future explainability layer, but not required before core SLA support is in place.

========================================
THE STRATEGY - SCAFFOLDING THE TRUTH
========================================

Key idea:
- For the May 24 shadow pilots, the goal is not perfect enterprise automation.
- The goal is to ensure the system can see real operational columns, respect them, and explain the consequences.

Most important product principle from this discussion:
- Even if the AI cannot find a perfect route, it is more credible if it explains why the route is infeasible than if it silently ignores the constraint.

Example of credible behavior:
- "I could not fit this stop because the 15-minute service time pushes the next stop into an SLA violation."

Why this is powerful:
- It aligns perfectly with the explainable-AI identity of the product.
- It makes failure modes feel trustworthy instead of incompetent.

========================================
ADDITIONAL ADD-ON FEATURES (ORGANIZED)
========================================

Purpose:
- Capture high-leverage add-on ideas discussed for post-shadow-pilot product differentiation.
- Keep timing explicit so current pilot scope does not get diluted.

----------------------------------------
A) BLAST RADIUS REROUTE (EXCEPTION MANAGEMENT)
----------------------------------------

Problem:
- A single truck delay can cause cascading downstream SLA failures.
- Dispatchers currently have to manually orchestrate rescue moves across other vehicles.

Feature concept:
- When delay is added on the map, instantly project downstream SLA failures.
- Propose multi-vehicle rescue plan with explanation.

Example output:
- "Truck 4 delayed by 90 mins; 3 SLA failures predicted. Move Stops B and C to Truck 7. Reason: Truck 7 is 4 miles away, has capacity, rescues all 3 SLAs, adds only 12 mins."

Build timing:
- Later (Phase 2+), not a May 24 blocker.

Claim confidence:
- High value and strong enterprise relevance; requires live delay state, cross-vehicle reassignment, and richer SLA modeling.

----------------------------------------
B) DRIVER EMPATHY SCORE (RETENTION + FATIGUE SIGNAL)
----------------------------------------

Problem:
- Dispatch decisions that ignore fatigue and home-time predictability can increase churn risk.

Feature concept:
- Add a "Driver Impact" explanation tag in route decision evidence.
- Example tag:
  - "Driver fatigue avoided: keeps assigned driver under shift threshold and returns earlier."

Build timing:
- Later (Phase 2+), after HOS/driver-state fundamentals are reliable.

Claim confidence:
- Strong buyer narrative; do not over-claim retention impact without pilot data.

----------------------------------------
C) PLANNED VS ACTUAL REPLAY (TRUST + OVERRIDE LEARNING)
----------------------------------------

Problem:
- Teams override the algorithm without clear feedback loop on whether override helped or hurt.

Feature concept:
- Weekly scorecard comparing AI suggestion vs dispatcher override vs actual GPS outcomes.
- Report both wins and losses with quantified impact.

Build timing:
- Later (Phase 2+), requires actual telemetry and override history.

Claim confidence:
- Strong trust mechanic; should be evidence-led, not punitive.

----------------------------------------
D) CUSTOMER-FACING SHIELD (EXPLAINED ETA UPDATES)
----------------------------------------

Problem:
- Silent ETA changes trigger "Where is my order?" call volume.

Feature concept:
- Push sanitized explainability payload to customer tracking/WhatsApp channel.
- Example:
  - "ETA updated to 4:15 PM. Reason: rerouted to avoid major accident; fastest safe alternative active."

Build timing:
- Later (Phase 2+), after stable eventing + notification path exists.

Claim confidence:
- High practical value; avoid hard call-volume/cost claims unless measured in pilot.

========================================
ADD-ON PRIORITY ORDER (POST SHADOW PILOT)
========================================

1. Blast Radius Reroute
2. Planned vs Actual Replay
3. Customer-Facing Shield
4. Driver Empathy Score

Reason for ordering:
- First two deepen core dispatch intelligence and trust loop directly.
- Third extends explainability to external communication.
- Fourth becomes most credible after stronger HOS + driver-state modeling.

========================================
FACT-CHECKING GUIDELINE FOR PITCH USE
========================================

Use strong certainty:
- FMCSA HOS rule framing and legality context.
- Operational pain from cascading delays and exception handling.
- Algorithmic aversion as a real trust challenge.

Use cautious language unless measured in own pilot:
- Exact WISMO percentages and per-call cost.
- Exact driver replacement cost ranges for all fleets.
- Guaranteed churn reduction or fixed hours saved.

Recommended wording pattern:
- "Can reduce" / "typically" / "often" instead of "always" or hard universal numbers.
